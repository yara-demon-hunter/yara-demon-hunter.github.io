— Zeph!

Oskar shouted as he walked among the men.

The fog was already covering almost everything.

The woodcutters were gathered near the wagons, some carrying tools, others hurriedly trying to stow the logs they had cut.

Three wagons.

Two filled with firewood.

The third carried nets, fish, tools, and a few supplies collected during the day.

The horses were restless.

One of them was pulling at the reins, snorting nervously.

Oskar tried to step forward.

— I’m going to get him.

The oldest woodcutter grabbed his arm.

— No.

— Let go!

— You’re not going.

— He’s inside! I’m going to bring my friend back!

— You’ll just be another one lost in the forest!

— The rule exists for a reason — the old man said.

— To hell with the rule!

— No.

The answer came from another man.

— You know what happens when someone wanders away from the group after the bell.

Oskar stared at the men.

No one seemed willing to step into the fog.

— So you’re just going to leave him there?

No one answered.

Silence weighed heavy among them.

Until the old man took a deep breath.

— We’re not leaving Zeph.

He pointed down the path toward the village.

— We’re going back to get help while we can still see the way back!

— And what if he isn’t there when we return?

Everyone fell silent.

— No! We are going to get help, Oskar.

Oskar opened his mouth to reply, but couldn’t.

One of the woodcutters raised a hand to his head.

— Wait...

He swayed slightly.

— I’m feeling kind of dizzy.

Another man frowned.

— Me too.

He ran a hand across his forehead and took a deep breath.

— My head... feels light.

Oskar looked at the two of them.

— What is happening?

Then...

A laugh emerged from inside the fog.

— Hehehehehe...

Everyone froze.

It was a woman's laugh.

Low.

High-pitched.

Distant.

One of the men raised his head.

— What was that?

No one answered.

Another laugh sounded.

This time, from a different direction.

— Hihihihihi...

The men looked at each other.

Then a third one answered.

Further away.

The silence died.

Now there were laughs everywhere.

To the left.

To the right.

Behind the trees.

They were different voices.

Laughing with each other.

As if they were hiding all around the men.

The horses began to panic.

One of them pulled violently at the reins and neighed.

— He’s going to break free!

Another horse began stomping its hooves against the ground.

— By God...

— What’s wrong with them?

The animal backed away, eyes wide, snorting in desperation.

One of the woodcutters turned pale.

— The horses...

He looked at the others.

— They can feel it.

— Feel what?

The man tightened his grip on his axe's handle.

A new burst of laughter cut through the fog.

This time, so close that a few men took a step back.

The youngest man crossed himself.

The word came out louder.

Another began to pray.

— Our Father who art in heaven...

— Pray louder — someone whispered.

— Hail Mary, full of grace...

Two more men joined in.

Their voices blended together.

— Deliver us from evil...

— Protect us tonight...

— For God's sake...

Axes were raised.

One after another.

Not to attack.

But because no one wanted to be unarmed.

Oskar looked at everyone in disbelief.

— Are you all going crazy?

One of the laughs echoed again.

Now there were several.

Overlapping.

Some sounded distant.

Others felt as if they were right behind them.

Oskar spun around.

Nothing.

Only the fog.

Oskar stepped forward.

— Don't play with me! I'm going to get him!

Two men grabbed his arms.

— Let me go!

— You’re not going in!

— HE IS IN THERE!

Oskar tried to break free.

— I’m not abandoning my friend!

— Do you want to die?

— Let go of me!

He thrashed against them.

A third man held him back.

— We need to go!

— NO!

For a second...

The laughter stopped.

All of it.

At the exact same time.

The men froze.

Then, from within the fog...

a female voice whispered:

— Zeph... He is already dead.

— Liar! You monster! — he raged.

Oskar stopped fighting.

The voice had come from very far away.

Another answered it.

— Zeph...

And another, and another...

The old man closed his eyes.

— To the wagons.

— But—

— NOW!

The men ran.

— Leave the tools!

— No! Take the axes!

— Grab the torches!

— And the horses!

Oskar was still resisting.

When a fourth woodcutter helped drag him to one of the wagons.

*I will come back for you, my friend.*

Oskar was forced into the wagon.

One of the men grabbed him by the shoulders.

— You are coming with us. If we stay here, no one goes back for anyone.

Oskar seemed to realize there was nothing he could do at that moment to help his friend.

— Let’s go, boys!

The three wagons began to move.

Oskar looked back at the forest.

*I'm sorry, my friend, there is nothing I can do...*

Then...

A laugh echoed behind them.

Then another.

And another.

Until the entire forest seemed to laugh.

The horses bolted, cutting through the fog ahead.

The wheels crushed the damp dirt.

The men held their axes tightly.

Some kept praying.

No one looked back.

Except Oskar.

He looked.

And, for a brief instant, he saw silhouettes inside the fog.

Watching.

Smiling.

Then the fog closed completely.

Everything vanished.

But the laughter continued.

Even when the forest was already far behind.

Even when the wagons had already taken the path to the village.

And, far behind them...

Dong.

The bell rang again.

This time... It lost itself in the void of the forest.
