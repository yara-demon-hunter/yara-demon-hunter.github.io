    <h1>The Bell of Gudran</h1>

    <p>In Gudran's Village, the bell is more than a way to mark the passing of the day. Its sound tells people working in the forest when to return and warns the settlement that danger may be near.</p>

    <h2>A rule shaped by the forest</h2>
    <p>Woodcutters and fishermen depend on the forest and its stream for their work. But the weather can turn, the birds can fall silent, and mist can close in among the trees. The village's rule is simple: when the bell rings, everyone must leave the woods; no one is permitted to enter after it sounds.</p>
    <p>The rule draws a line between the familiar work of daylight and the unknown beyond the village walls. It is not a guarantee of safety: it is a warning learned through experience.</p>

    <h2>The day the bell rang late</h2>
    <p>The opening chapters show how much the villagers rely on this signal. When the bell sounds late, people in the forest do not know whether to keep working or head home. That delay becomes part of the circumstances surrounding Zeph's disappearance.</p>
